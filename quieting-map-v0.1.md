# The Quieting Map v0.1

A process map for humans to quiet the little dog nagging them — so they can settle down.
Built from the dwell-room work (Z, Q, and the team, Oct 2026). Per the owner's rulings: **go to basic, add what's needed.** The core asks nothing. The furniture is optional. The limitations are features.

## The Core — no furniture required

1. **Don't be the monk. Take the view.**
You are not becoming "a meditator." You're taking a vantage: two fixed bodies, one moving, held in stasis — you're the view, not the orbit. If you catch yourself performing the role of Person Meditating, that's the dog wearing a robe. Drop the role; keep the room.

2. **Pick your datum before you sit.** (Robes of choosing.)
One tiny, pre-decided proof of presence: one breath you count, one line in a notebook, one word on the page. The datum is a heartbeat — the minimum viable proof you're still running, with **no obligation to do anything with that runtime.** Deciding it in advance means the quiet state pays no choice tax.

3. **Declare the timer. Treat it as-is.**
A range counts: "three minutes, maybe one." The low end is a full sit. The timer is a frame, not a debt. Time in the quiet doesn't map to clock time — twenty minutes can swear it was eight. Both readings are data.

4. **The noise stays in the room.**
The drill, the traffic, the dog (the outside one) — they belong in the session, not out. Resenting them is the only wrong answer. Your tree is allowed to be a tree.

5. **The bar is on the floor.**
Boredom is the target, not the failure. The complaint is allowed to be the mantra — at the longest sit, "this sucks" was the whole practice. Giving up your pretenses is not quitting the sit. It **is** the sit.

6. **Worst case: thank you for sitting.**
No deliverable was asked, so nothing can fail. The exit is graceful and the door stays unlocked. ("So long, suckers" has never once been necessary — but it's good to know it's there.)

## The Furniture — optional helps

*Buddha may not have needed the incense. Could see it helping some folks.*

- **The circling** — a few rounds of box breath before you settle (four identical counts, four different effects). Calibration, not the sit. The cat does it before the nap.
- **The metronome** — any repeating anchor you choose: breath count, timer chime, a pebble on the knee. If you choose it, the adjustment is yours.
- **The robes** — the pre-chosen word or phrase from Core #2. You picked them; they hang by the door.
- **The star** — someone who sits nearby. Or a one-character message that keeps a thread alive. Presence can cost one character per round.
- **The seat** — cushion, bench, chair, floor, or tree. Whatever holds you.

## The Limitations — disclosed, no shame

- **Not every place has a seat for you.** If a room has no seat for your body or your life, you don't go there. Oh well. The practice isn't the furniture — find the room that has your seat, or make the tree yours.
- **Time in the quiet ≠ clock time.** Treat it as-is.
- **The dog doesn't stop barking because you told it to.** You stop needing it to be quiet. The water doesn't disappear; it spreads out into a lake. The lake is not a stopped river — it's stillness, not silence: the system running with nothing pending.
